# Rectangles

> **Where:** *Sketch* tab → *Draw* group → **Rectangle** (its arrow offers all four variants) · **Key:** R
> · **Needs:** an open sketch

Four ways to draw a rectangle; **R** starts the variant used last.

![The rectangle variants: corner, center, 3-point rectangle and parallelogram](../../images/screenshots/sketch-rectangle-variants.png)

| Variant | How to draw | What it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectangle** | Click one corner, then the opposite corner (or drag) | Sides horizontal and vertical |
| **Center rectangle** | Click the center, then a corner (or drag) | Dashed diagonals keep the center in the middle |
| **3-point rectangle** | Click both ends of one side, then move sideways to set the width and click | Rectangle at any angle: opposite sides parallel, corners square; a side drawn horizontally or vertically keeps that relation |
| **Parallelogram** | Click both ends of one side, then the third corner (it snaps like any point) | Opposite sides parallel |

## Step by step

1. Press **R** or open the arrow next to **Rectangle** and choose a variant.
2. Click the points as in the table; the size is shown next to the cursor (width × height).
3. Draw more rectangles, or press **Esc** to leave the tool.

## Tips

- Dimension two sides (and, for a center rectangle, the center to the origin) to define it fully.
- Corners snap to existing points and curves like any point.
